\begin{abstract}
Many forecasting tasks ask for the probability that a process ends above a threshold, given only the part of its path seen so far, and the spread of that process switches between activity levels.
We release \texttt{regimix}, which fits a label-free hidden Markov model (a model with unobserved switching states) to unit-time scaled lookback segments, mixes per-regime Gaussian exceedance probabilities by the filtered regime weights, and calibrates the mixture on out-of-fold outputs.
On EUR/USD 1-minute episodes, regimix ($K=\nCfgK$) beats the one-regime pooled probit by \nFxKoneAbsD{} nats of log-loss (paired moving-block bootstrap SE \nFxKoneSE), but it ties a continuous realised-volatility probit (\nFxRvD, SE \nFxRvSE) and loses to a HAR-style probit (\nFxHarD, SE \nFxHarSE).
Across \nCfgNData{} public datasets it ties or loses to the best volatility probit, and appending its outputs to gradient boosting changes log-loss by \nFxStackD{} / \nEqStackD{} / \nMfStackD{} (SE \nFxStackSE{} / \nEqStackSE{} / \nMfStackSE), never better.
Every number comes from one held-out tail of time (the last \nCfgTestPct\,\% per dataset, \nFxSeeds{} seeds), with standard errors from bootstraps over time blocks or whole series.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem}
\label{sec:problem}

An episode $i$ is a scalar process $X$ observed up to a decision time $t_i$, a threshold $k_i$ and a remaining horizon $\tau_i>0$.
The label is
\begin{equation}
  y_i = \mathbb{1}\{X(t_i+\tau_i) > k_i\},
  \label{eq:label}
\end{equation}
and the goal is a calibrated $P(y_i=1)$ from the path before $t_i$.
Here $\mathbb{1}\{\cdot\}$ is 1 when the event holds and 0 otherwise.
The plain answer is a probit: a Gaussian tail probability whose spread comes from the recent path.
That works when the spread is stable.
It hurts when the spread jumps between a quiet and an active level faster than a long realised-volatility window (the summed squared recent increments) adapts.

Our hypothesis was that a filtered regime, read off a few lookback windows of different length, reacts within one window and so beats a slow volatility estimate.
The filter is Hamilton's \citep{hamilton1989} and the regime-weighted normal tail is standard in regime-switching return models \citep{hardy2001}.
We claim neither.
The delta is a label-free fit over many short episodes instead of one long series, a cross-fitted calibration, a variance-floor fix to the upstream library \citep{hmmlearn}, and diagnostics that say when the regimes are not real.
The result is mostly negative, and we report it as such.

\section{The change}
\label{sec:change}

Pick lookback edges $0=e_0<e_1<\dots<e_S$.
Segment $s$ is the increment over one window, scaled to unit time:
\begin{align}
  z_{i,s} &= \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{X(t_i-e_{s-1})-X(t_i-e_s)}{\sqrt{e_s-e_{s-1}}},\,-c,\,c\right), \label{eq:seg}\\
  w_i &= \alpha_{i,S}\,A,\qquad \alpha_{i,S}(j)=P(r_S=j\mid z_{i,1},\dots,z_{i,S}), \label{eq:filter}\\
  \tilde p_i &= \sum_{j=1}^{K} w_i(j)\,\Phi\!\left(\frac{d_i+\mu_j\tau_i}{\max(\sigma_j,\sigma_{\min})\sqrt{\tau_i}}\right),\qquad d_i = X(t_i)-k_i, \label{eq:mix}\\
  p_i &= \operatorname{sigmoid}\!\big(a+b\,\operatorname{logit}\tilde p_i\big). \label{eq:platt}
\end{align}
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning \\
\midrule
$z_{i,s}$ & segment $s$ of episode $i$, oldest first; i.i.d.\ under one Brownian regime \\
$c$ & optional clip on extreme segments \\
$r_s$, $K$ & hidden regime at segment $s$; number of regimes \\
$A$, $\alpha_{i,S}$ & transition matrix; filtered regime probabilities after the last segment \\
$w_i$ & regime weights for the horizon (one-step prediction, \texttt{next\_step}) \\
$\mu_j$, $\sigma_j^2$ & drift and variance of regime $j$ per unit time; $\mu_j=0$ by default \\
$\sigma_{\min}$ & spread floor, \nCfgSigmaMin{} by default \\
$\Phi$ & standard normal cumulative distribution function \\
$a$, $b$ & Platt parameters \citep{platt1999,lin2007}, fitted on out-of-fold $\operatorname{logit}\tilde p$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The hidden Markov model (HMM) has $K$ Gaussian states and is fitted by Baum--Welch \citep{baum1970} on all episodes' segment sequences, with no labels.
Equation~\eqref{eq:filter} is the forward pass, batched over episodes.
Equation~\eqref{eq:mix} averages one random-walk tail probability per regime.
Equation~\eqref{eq:platt} is the only place labels enter, through two parameters.
The calibrator is fitted on outputs from $\nCfgFolds$ contiguous time-block folds, then the HMM is refitted on all training rows.
The estimator also returns $w_i$ and $\operatorname{logit}\tilde p_i$, so a downstream learner can use the regimes as inputs.

The mixture is exact under a stated assumption and the label path is clean.
Both are claimed, so both are proved (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).

\begin{lemma}[exact mixture]
\label{lem:mix}
If, given the horizon regime $r=j$, $X(t_i+\tau_i)-X(t_i)\sim\mathcal N(\mu_j\tau_i,\sigma_j^2\tau_i)$ independently of $z_{i,1..S}$, $P(r=j\mid z_i)=w_i(j)$ and $\sigma_j\ge\sigma_{\min}$, then $\tilde p_i=P(y_i=1\mid z_i)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[no test label enters]
\label{lem:leak}
If no training label reads a value of $X$ inside the held-out period, then $p_i$ for a held-out episode depends on labels only through training labels.
\end{lemma}

The second part of the change is the variance floor.
hmmlearn documents \texttt{min\_covar} as a floor on the covariance but applies it only at initialisation.
On data with many exact zeros one state then collapses onto a spike at zero.
Its likelihood grows without bound and its posterior turns into a one-bit gate.
\texttt{FlooredGaussianHMM} applies the floor after every M-step (the parameter-update half of EM):
\begin{equation}
  v_j \leftarrow \max\!\left(v_j^{\mathrm{EM}},\,m\right).
  \label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
Here $v_j^{\mathrm{EM}}$ is the unconstrained variance update and $m$ is \texttt{min\_covar} (\nCfgMinCovar{} by default).

\begin{lemma}[monotone EM]
\label{lem:floor}
For diagonal or spherical covariances, update~\eqref{eq:floor} is the exact maximiser of the M-step objective over $\{v\ge m\}$, so the log-likelihood never decreases.
\end{lemma}

The diagnostics turn the conditions for a gain into warnings.
With fitted variances $v_j$ and self-transition probabilities $A_{jj}$:
\begin{equation}
  \rho = \frac{\min_j v_j}{m},\qquad D_j = \frac{1}{1-A_{jj}},\qquad R = \frac{\max_j\sigma_j}{\min_j\sigma_j}.
  \label{eq:diag}
\end{equation}
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning \\
\midrule
$\rho$ & collapse ratio: smallest variance over the floor; near 1 means a state sits on the floor \\
$D_j$ & mean dwell of regime $j$ in segments; warns below \nCfgDwellMin \\
$R$ & spread ratio between the widest and narrowest regime; warns below \nCfgSpreadMin \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The report also lists the share of exactly flat segments, the number of distinct posterior values (a gate has two) and the permutation importance of each segment.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data.}
\nCfgNData{} public datasets, chosen to have switching activity levels.
\emph{EUR/USD 1-minute} bars from HistData, \nCfgFxYearA--\nCfgFxYearB: at a random minute each hour, $P(\text{price in } \nCfgFxH\text{ min} > \text{price} + u)$ with $u\in\{-\nCfgFxPip, 0, +\nCfgFxPip\}$ pips; edges \nCfgFxEdges{} minutes.
\emph{US large-cap daily} closes for \nCfgEqTickers{} tickers, \nCfgEqYearA--\nCfgEqYearB, one decision day in \nCfgEqEvery{} per ticker: $P(\text{close in } \nCfgEqH \text{ days} > \text{close}\times(1+u))$, $u\in\{-\nCfgEqPct, 0, +\nCfgEqPct\}\,\%$; edges \nCfgEqEdges{} days.
\emph{M4 hourly} \citep{makridakis2020}, one decision in \nCfgMfEvery{} hours: $P(\log y_{t+h}>\log y_t+u)$ for $h\in\{\nCfgMfHa,\nCfgMfHb\}$ hours and $u=c\,\hat\sigma_1\sqrt h$, $c\in\{-\nCfgMfC,0,\nCfgMfC\}$; edges \nCfgMfEdges{} hours.
The held-out set is the last \nCfgTestPct\,\% of time (per series for M4), never shuffled.
Training rows whose label reads the held-out period are dropped, an embargo of one horizon.
Sizes are \nFxNTrain{} / \nFxNTest, \nEqNTrain{} / \nEqNTest{} and \nMfNTrain{} / \nMfNTest{} training / held-out episodes.
Each series is scaled by the spread of its one-step increments on the training part.

\paragraph{Arms.}
All arms see the same segments, distances and horizons, and all get a Platt map fitted on training rows.
\emph{regimix} is the defaults: $K=\nCfgK$, \texttt{n\_iter} \nCfgNIter, \texttt{tol} \nCfgTol, floor on, next step on, drift off.
The \emph{one-regime pooled probit} is $K=\nCfgKone$.
The \emph{realised-vol probit} uses the realised variance per unit time over the whole lookback.
The \emph{HAR-style probit} regresses log realised variance over the horizon on log realised variance at each lookback window \citep{corsi2009,andersen2003}.
The \emph{gate ablation} replaces $w_i$ by a one-hot ``last segment large''.
\emph{Logistic regression} and \emph{histogram gradient boosting} \citep{pedregosa2011} get $(Z,|Z|,d,\tau,d/\sqrt\tau)$.
\emph{Stock EM} is pristine hmmlearn 0.3.3 \citep{hmmlearn} without the floor.
The stacking test \citep{wolpert1992} appends out-of-fold $w_i$ and $\operatorname{logit}\tilde p_i$ to the boosting inputs.
The benchmark arm equals the public \texttt{RegimeExceedanceClassifier} with a maximum logit difference of \nFxApiDiff.

\paragraph{Metric and error.}
Log-loss in nats, averaged over \nFxSeeds{} seeds; the paired difference per episode is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_i = \ell(p_i^{\text{regimix}},y_i)-\ell(p_i^{\text{arm}},y_i),\qquad \ell(p,y)=-y\log p-(1-y)\log(1-p).
  \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
Negative $\bar\Delta$ means regimix is better.
Episodes in one time block share a path, so a per-episode SE is too small (FX against $K=\nCfgKone$: \nFxKoneNaiveSE{} against \nFxKoneSE).
We sum $\Delta_i$ within blocks and run a moving-block bootstrap \citep{kunsch1989} with \nCfgBoot{} draws over \nFxNUnits{} FX days and \nEqNUnits{} equity dates.
The block length is the first lag at which the autocorrelation of block means falls inside $2/\sqrt n$.
M4 series are independent, so we resample \nMfNUnits{} whole series.
A Newey--West SE \citep{neweywest1987} on block means is kept as a check.
We call a difference a tie when its 95\,\% interval covers zero.
Calibration is the expected calibration error over \nCfgBins{} bins (ECE, the mean gap between predicted and observed frequency) \citep{guo2017}.

\paragraph{Pre-registration.}
The \nCfgNArms{} arms of the main run were written down before any held-out number existed.
After the main run lost to the volatility probits we tried \nCfgNVariants{} post-hoc variants on the same tail, about \nCfgNPostCmp{} comparisons, and kept a variant only if it won on all \nCfgNData{} datasets.

\section{Limits}
\label{sec:limits}

The main result is a tie with, or a loss to, a continuous volatility probit, and no tuning we tried changed that.
In the improvement ledger it ``lost a comparison'' and stays reopenable: the winning baseline is label-light and closed-form, so it meets every design constraint.
A gain would need something the level misses, such as regime switching inside the horizon \citep{hardy2001} or longer horizons; neither was tested.
Each dataset has one held-out tail and \nFxSeeds{} seeds, and the post-hoc variants were scored on that same tail.
The variance-floor fix has only synthetic evidence here.
The equity universe has survivorship bias (today's large caps with full history), which hits every arm alike.
On M4 the M4 rows speak only about the probit family.
Behind \texttt{drift=True} the segment mean is drift per $\sqrt{\text{time}}$ while Equation~\eqref{eq:mix} reads it per unit time; drift is off by default for that reason.

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\paragraph{Lemma~\ref{lem:mix}.}
\textbf{Given:}
1) law of total probability over a partition $\{r=j\}_{j=1}^{K}$;
2) given $r=j$, the increment $U=X(t_i+\tau_i)-X(t_i)\sim\mathcal N(\mu_j\tau_i,\sigma_j^2\tau_i)$, independent of $z_i$;
3) $P(r=j\mid z_i)=w_i(j)$;
4) $\Phi(-x)=1-\Phi(x)$.

\textbf{Choose:} the event $E=\{y_i=1\}=\{X(t_i)+U>k_i\}=\{U>-d_i\}$ and the partition of rule 1.

\textbf{Need to prove:} $P(E\mid z_i)=\sum_j w_i(j)\,\Phi\big((d_i+\mu_j\tau_i)/(\sigma_j\sqrt{\tau_i})\big)$.
\begin{align*}
P(E\mid z_i) &= \textstyle\sum_j P(E\mid r=j, z_i)\,P(r=j\mid z_i) && \text{rule 1}\\
             &= \textstyle\sum_j P(E\mid r=j)\,w_i(j) && \text{independence in rule 2, rule 3}\\
P(E\mid r=j) &= P\!\left(\tfrac{U-\mu_j\tau_i}{\sigma_j\sqrt{\tau_i}} > \tfrac{-d_i-\mu_j\tau_i}{\sigma_j\sqrt{\tau_i}}\right) && \text{standardise $U$, rule 2}\\
             &= 1-\Phi\!\left(\tfrac{-d_i-\mu_j\tau_i}{\sigma_j\sqrt{\tau_i}}\right) && \text{definition of $\Phi$}\\
             &= \Phi\!\left(\tfrac{d_i+\mu_j\tau_i}{\sigma_j\sqrt{\tau_i}}\right) && \text{rule 4}
\end{align*}
\textbf{Precondition:} Equation~\eqref{eq:mix} uses $\max(\sigma_j,\sigma_{\min})$, which equals $\sigma_j$ because $\sigma_j\ge\sigma_{\min}$.
\textbf{Therefore,} substitution gives $\tilde p_i=P(y_i=1\mid z_i)$. \qed

\paragraph{Lemma~\ref{lem:floor}.}
\textbf{Given:}
1) generalised EM \citep{dempster1977}: if each update satisfies $Q(\theta^{\text{new}}\mid\theta^{\text{old}})\ge Q(\theta^{\text{old}}\mid\theta^{\text{old}})$, the log-likelihood does not decrease;
2) for diagonal or spherical covariances $Q$ separates into one term per state and coordinate, and the variance term has the form $q(v)=-\tfrac12\,(\nu\log v + s/v)$ with weight $\nu>0$ and scatter $s>0$ (a conjugate variance prior only changes $\nu$ and $s$);
3) the mean update does not depend on $v$.

\textbf{Choose:} one variance term $q$ and the feasible set $v\ge m$.

\textbf{Need to prove:} $\arg\max_{v\ge m} q(v)=\max(s/\nu,\,m)$.
\begin{align*}
q'(v) &= -\tfrac12\left(\tfrac{\nu}{v}-\tfrac{s}{v^2}\right) && \text{derivative of rule 2}\\
      &= \tfrac{\nu}{2v^2}\left(\tfrac{s}{\nu}-v\right) && \text{factor out } \tfrac{\nu}{2v^2}>0
\end{align*}
So $q'>0$ for $v<s/\nu$ and $q'<0$ for $v>s/\nu$: $q$ is unimodal with peak $v^\ast=s/\nu=v^{\mathrm{EM}}$.
If $v^\ast\ge m$ the peak is feasible and is the maximiser.
If $v^\ast<m$ then $q$ is decreasing on $[m,\infty)$ and the maximiser is $m$.
Both cases give $\max(v^\ast,m)$, which is update~\eqref{eq:floor}.

\textbf{Precondition:} rule 1 needs $\theta^{\text{old}}$ feasible, so that it is a candidate of the constrained maximisation.
The initial variances carry $+m$, so they are feasible.
Every later variance is the output of~\eqref{eq:floor}, so it is $\ge m$.
By induction every iterate is feasible.

\textbf{Therefore,} by rule 3 the means keep their usual update, each variance gets its exact constrained maximiser, $Q$ does not decrease, and by rule 1 neither does the log-likelihood. \qed

\paragraph{Lemma~\ref{lem:leak}.}
\textbf{Given:}
1) $\theta=\mathrm{fit}(Z_{\text{train}})$, Baum--Welch on segments only;
2) $(a,b)=\mathrm{Platt}\big(\{\operatorname{logit}\tilde p_j^{\text{oof}}, y_j\}_{j\in\text{train}}\big)$, where each out-of-fold output comes from an HMM fitted on segments only;
3) for a held-out episode, $p_i=g(z_i,d_i,\tau_i;\theta,a,b)$ by Equations~\eqref{eq:filter}--\eqref{eq:platt}.

\textbf{Need to prove:} $p_i$ depends on labels only through $\{y_j\}_{j\in\text{train}}$.
\begin{align*}
p_i &= g(z_i,d_i,\tau_i;\,\theta,\,a,\,b) && \text{rule 3}\\
    &= g\big(z_i,d_i,\tau_i;\,\mathrm{fit}(Z_{\text{train}}),\,\mathrm{Platt}(\{\tilde p_j^{\text{oof}},y_j\}_{j\in\text{train}})\big) && \text{rules 1 and 2}
\end{align*}
The only label symbols on the right are $y_j$ with $j\in\text{train}$.

\textbf{Precondition:} a training label must not itself carry held-out information.
A training label reads $X(t_j+\tau_j)$; rows with $t_j+\tau_j$ inside the held-out period are dropped (the embargo of Section~\ref{sec:setup}), so every remaining $y_j$ is a function of $X$ before the held-out period.

\textbf{Therefore,} no held-out label enters $p_i$. The out-of-fold step is not needed for this; it only makes the calibrator see raw outputs from an HMM that did not fit those rows, as at test time. \qed
