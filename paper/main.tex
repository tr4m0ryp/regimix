\begin{abstract}
We want the probability that a process ends above a threshold, given part of a path whose spread switches between levels.
We release \texttt{regimix}: a hidden Markov model (unobserved switching states) fitted without labels to scaled recent increments, whose filtered state weights mix per-state Gaussian tail probabilities; it fixes a variance-floor bug in hmmlearn 0.3.3.
The finding is negative: the states beat a one-state model but not a plain volatility estimate.
On \nFxNTest{} held-out EUR/USD 1-minute episodes, regimix ($K=\nCfgK$) beats the one-state pooled probit by \nFxKoneAbsD{} nats of log-loss (paired block-bootstrap SE \nFxKoneSE), ties a realised-volatility probit (\nFxRvD, SE \nFxRvSE; positive means regimix is worse) and loses to a HAR-style probit (\nFxHarD, SE \nFxHarSE); pristine hmmlearn 0.3.3 EM gives identical numbers.
On all \nCfgNData{} public datasets it ties or loses to the best volatility probit, and its outputs never improve gradient boosting (\nFxStackD{} / \nEqStackD{} / \nMfStackD{} nats, SE \nFxStackSE{} / \nEqStackSE{} / \nMfStackSE).
SEs bootstrap over time blocks or series; one held-out tail per dataset.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem}
\label{sec:problem}

An episode $i$ is a scalar process $X$ observed up to a decision time $t_i$, a threshold $k_i$ and a remaining horizon $\tau_i>0$.
The label is
\begin{equation}
  y_i = \mathbf{1}\{X(t_i+\tau_i) > k_i\},
  \label{eq:label}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{1}\{\cdot\}$ the indicator of the event, and the goal is a calibrated $P(y_i=1)$ from the path before $t_i$.
The plain answer is a probit, a Gaussian tail probability whose width comes from the recent path, which works when volatility is stable but fails when activity jumps between a quiet and a busy level faster than a long realised-volatility window (the summed squared recent increments) can follow.

We expected a filtered regime, read off a few lookback windows, to react within one window and beat a slow volatility estimate.
The filter is Hamilton's \citep{hamilton1989} and the regime-weighted normal tail is standard in regime-switching return models \citep{hardy2001}; we claim neither.
The delta is a label-free fit over many short episodes instead of one long series, a cross-fitted calibration, a variance-floor fix to the upstream library \citep{hmmlearn}, and diagnostics that say when the regimes are not real.
The result is mostly negative, and we report it as such.

\section{The change}
\label{sec:change}

Pick lookback edges $0=e_0<e_1<\dots<e_S$.
Segment $s$ is the increment over one window, scaled to unit time:
\begin{align}
  z_{i,s} &= \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{X(t_i-e_{s-1})-X(t_i-e_s)}{\sqrt{e_s-e_{s-1}}},\,-c,\,c\right), \label{eq:seg}\\
  w_i &= \alpha_{i,S}\,A,\qquad \alpha_{i,S}(j)=P(r_S=j\mid z_{i,1},\dots,z_{i,S}), \label{eq:filter}\\
  \tilde p_i &= \sum_{j=1}^{K} w_i(j)\,\Phi\!\left(\frac{d_i+\mu_j\tau_i}{\max(\sigma_j,\sigma_{\min})\sqrt{\tau_i}}\right),\qquad d_i = X(t_i)-k_i, \label{eq:mix}\\
  p_i &= \operatorname{sigmoid}\!\big(a+b\,\operatorname{logit}\tilde p_i\big). \label{eq:platt}
\end{align}
\begin{center}
\small
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning & Symbol & Meaning \\
\midrule
$z_{i,s}$ & segment $s$, oldest first & $c$ & optional clip \\
$r_s$, $K$ & regime at segment $s$; regime count & $A$ & transition matrix \\
$\alpha_{i,S}$ & filtered regime probabilities & $w_i$ & horizon regime weights \\
$\mu_j$, $\sigma_j^2$ & drift, variance per unit time ($\mu_j=0$) & $\sigma_{\min}$ & spread floor (\nCfgSigmaMin) \\
$\Phi$ & normal CDF & $a$, $b$ & Platt parameters \citep{platt1999,lin2007} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

We fit the hidden Markov model (HMM) with $K$ Gaussian states by Baum--Welch \citep{baum1970} on the segment sequences of all episodes, without labels.
Equation~\eqref{eq:filter} is its forward pass, batched over episodes, moved one step ahead by $A$ (next-step weighting), and Equation~\eqref{eq:mix} averages one random-walk tail probability per regime.
Labels enter only in Equation~\eqref{eq:platt}, through two parameters that we fit on outputs from $\nCfgFolds$ contiguous time-block folds before the HMM is refitted on all training rows.
The estimator also returns $w_i$ and $\operatorname{logit}\tilde p_i$, so that a downstream learner can use the regimes as inputs.
We claim three properties, and Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} proves each of them.

\begin{lemma}[exact mixture]
\label{lem:mix}
If, given the horizon regime $r=j$, $X(t_i+\tau_i)-X(t_i)\sim\mathcal N(\mu_j\tau_i,\sigma_j^2\tau_i)$ independently of $z_{i,1..S}$, $P(r=j\mid z_i)=w_i(j)$ and $\sigma_j\ge\sigma_{\min}$, then $\tilde p_i=P(y_i=1\mid z_i)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[no test label enters]
\label{lem:leak}
If no training label reads a value of $X$ inside the held-out period, then $p_i$ for a held-out episode depends on labels only through training labels.
\end{lemma}

The second part of the change is a variance floor: hmmlearn documents \texttt{min\_covar} as a floor on the covariance but applies it only at initialisation, so on data with many exact zeros one state collapses onto a spike at zero.
Its likelihood then grows without bound and its posterior takes only two values, a one-bit gate.
Our \texttt{FlooredGaussianHMM} applies the floor after every M-step (the parameter-update half of EM):
\begin{equation}
  v_j \leftarrow \max\!\left(v_j^{\mathrm{EM}},\,m\right).
  \label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
Here $v_j^{\mathrm{EM}}$ is the unconstrained variance update and $m$ is \texttt{min\_covar} (\nCfgMinCovar{} by default).

\begin{lemma}[monotone EM]
\label{lem:floor}
For diagonal or spherical covariances, update~\eqref{eq:floor} is the exact maximiser of the M-step objective over $\{v\ge m\}$, so the log-likelihood never decreases.
\end{lemma}

We turn the conditions under which regimes can help into warnings, computed from the fitted variances $v_j$ and the self-transition probabilities $A_{jj}$:
\begin{equation}
  \rho = \frac{\min_j v_j}{m},\qquad D_j = \frac{1}{1-A_{jj}},\qquad R = \frac{\max_j\sigma_j}{\min_j\sigma_j}.
  \label{eq:diag}
\end{equation}
Here $\rho$ is the collapse ratio (near 1: a state sits on the floor), $D_j$ the mean dwell in segments and $R$ the spread ratio.
A dwell below \nCfgDwellMin{} segments or a spread ratio below \nCfgSpreadMin{} triggers a warning; the report also gives the share of exactly flat segments, the count of distinct posterior values (a gate has two) and per-segment permutation importance.
Appendix~\ref{app:use} gives the inputs, the four calls that apply regimix to any path, and the commands that reproduce every number here.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data.}
We use \nCfgNData{} public datasets, each chosen because its activity level switches.
\emph{EUR/USD 1-minute} bars from HistData, \nCfgFxYearA--\nCfgFxYearB: at a random minute each hour, $P(\text{price in } \nCfgFxH\text{ min} > \text{price} + u)$ with $u\in\{-\nCfgFxPip, 0, +\nCfgFxPip\}$ pips (1 pip $=0.0001$ in price).
\emph{US large-cap daily} closes for \nCfgEqTickers{} tickers, \nCfgEqYearA--\nCfgEqYearB, one decision day in \nCfgEqEvery{} per ticker: $P(\text{close in } \nCfgEqH \text{ days} > \text{close}\times(1+u))$, $u\in\{-\nCfgEqPct, 0, +\nCfgEqPct\}\,\%$.
\emph{M4 hourly}, the hourly series of the M4 forecasting competition \citep{makridakis2020}, one decision in \nCfgMfEvery{} hours: $P(\log y_{t+h}>\log y_t+u)$ for $h\in\{\nCfgMfHa,\nCfgMfHb\}$ hours and $u=c\,\hat\sigma_1\sqrt h$ ($\hat\sigma_1$ the one-step training spread), $c\in\{-\nCfgMfC,0,\nCfgMfC\}$. Appendix~\ref{app:variants} lists the lookback edges.
The held-out set is the last \nCfgTestPct\,\% of time (per series for M4) and is never shuffled; we drop every training row whose label reads into it, an embargo (a gap against look-ahead) of one horizon.
That leaves \nFxNTrain{} / \nFxNTest, \nEqNTrain{} / \nEqNTest{} and \nMfNTrain{} / \nMfNTest{} training / held-out episodes, each series scaled by the spread of its one-step training increments.

\paragraph{Arms.}
All arms see the same training rows, segments, distances and horizons, and we give each a Platt map fitted on training rows.
No arm gets a hyperparameter search: each runs fixed settings written down before the run.
\emph{regimix} runs its defaults ($K=\nCfgK$, \texttt{n\_iter} \nCfgNIter, \texttt{tol} \nCfgTol, floor and next-step weighting $w_i=\alpha_{i,S}A$ on, drift off), and the \emph{one-regime pooled probit}, our trivial baseline, is the same model with $K=\nCfgKone$: one constant spread.
The \emph{realised-vol probit} uses the realised variance per unit time over the lookback; the \emph{HAR-style probit} (heterogeneous autoregressive volatility) regresses log realised variance over the horizon on its value at each lookback window \citep{corsi2009,andersen2003}.
The \emph{gate ablation} replaces $w_i$ by a one-hot weight on the high-spread state when $|z_{i,S}|$ exceeds the training quantile that matches that state's stationary share.
\emph{Logistic regression} and \emph{histogram gradient boosting} \citep{pedregosa2011} get $(Z,|Z|,d,\tau,d/\sqrt\tau)$.
\emph{Pristine hmmlearn EM} is the upstream 0.3.3 M-step \citep{hmmlearn} without the floor (our batched E-step matches upstream to $10^{-14}$), and the stacking test \citep{wolpert1992} appends out-of-fold $w_i$ and $\operatorname{logit}\tilde p_i$ to the boosting inputs.

\paragraph{Metric and error.}
Log-loss in nats, averaged over \nFxSeeds{} seeds; the paired difference per episode is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_i = \ell(p_i^{\text{regimix}},y_i)-\ell(p_i^{\text{arm}},y_i),\qquad \ell(p,y)=-y\log p-(1-y)\log(1-p).
  \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
Negative $\bar\Delta$ means regimix is better.
Episodes in one time block share a path, so a per-episode SE is too small (FX against $K=\nCfgKone$: \nFxKoneNaiveSE{} against \nFxKoneSE).
We therefore sum $\Delta_i$ within blocks and run a moving-block bootstrap \citep{kunsch1989} (resampling runs of consecutive blocks) with \nCfgBoot{} draws over \nFxNUnits{} FX days and \nEqNUnits{} equity dates, with the block length set by the first lag at which the autocorrelation of block means falls inside $2/\sqrt n$.
M4 series are independent, so there we resample \nMfNUnits{} whole series (a Newey--West SE \citep{neweywest1987} is kept as a check).
We call a difference a tie when its \nCfgCI\,\% interval covers zero.
Calibration is the expected calibration error over \nCfgBins{} bins (ECE, the mean gap between predicted and observed frequency) \citep{guo2017}.
The \nCfgNArms{} arms of the main run were written down before any held-out number existed.
After the main run lost to the volatility probits we tried \nCfgNVariants{} post-hoc variants on the same tail, about \nCfgNPostCmp{} comparisons, and kept a variant only if it won on all \nCfgNData{} datasets.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

No dataset gives regimix a win over the best volatility probit (Table~\ref{tab:main}).
On EUR/USD the regimes pass both diagnostics: they persist \nFxDwellA{} and \nFxDwellB{} segments on average and their spreads differ \nFxSpreadRatio-fold. There regimix beats the pooled probit by \nFxKoneAbsD{} nats and the gate ablation by \nFxGateAbsD.

\section{Limits}
\label{sec:limits}

The idea lost a comparison and stays open: a realised-volatility probit needs no hidden states and fits two calibration parameters, so it meets every constraint we set, and here it reads the current volatility level as well as the states do.
To beat it, the states must carry what the level misses, such as a switch inside the horizon \citep{hardy2001}, which regimix does not model.
The evidence is thin: one held-out tail per dataset, \nFxSeeds{} seeds, post-hoc variants on that tail, a floor with only synthetic evidence, survivorship bias in the equities, M4 rows that speak only about probits, a drift unit mismatch (Appendix~\ref{app:variants}), and no ablation of the out-of-fold calibration or the pooled fit.

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\paragraph{Lemma~\ref{lem:mix}.}
\textbf{Given:}
1) law of total probability over a partition $\{r=j\}_{j=1}^{K}$;
2) given $r=j$, the increment $U=X(t_i+\tau_i)-X(t_i)\sim\mathcal N(\mu_j\tau_i,\sigma_j^2\tau_i)$, independent of $z_i$;
3) $P(r=j\mid z_i)=w_i(j)$;
4) $\Phi(-x)=1-\Phi(x)$.

\textbf{Choose:} the event $E=\{y_i=1\}=\{X(t_i)+U>k_i\}=\{U>-d_i\}$ and the partition of rule 1.

\textbf{Need to prove:} $P(E\mid z_i)=\sum_j w_i(j)\,\Phi\big((d_i+\mu_j\tau_i)/(\sigma_j\sqrt{\tau_i})\big)$.
\begin{align*}
P(E\mid z_i) &= \textstyle\sum_j P(E\mid r=j, z_i)\,P(r=j\mid z_i) && \text{rule 1}\\
             &= \textstyle\sum_j P(E\mid r=j)\,w_i(j) && \text{independence in rule 2, rule 3}\\
P(E\mid r=j) &= P\!\left(\tfrac{U-\mu_j\tau_i}{\sigma_j\sqrt{\tau_i}} > \tfrac{-d_i-\mu_j\tau_i}{\sigma_j\sqrt{\tau_i}}\right) && \text{standardise $U$, rule 2}\\
             &= 1-\Phi\!\left(\tfrac{-d_i-\mu_j\tau_i}{\sigma_j\sqrt{\tau_i}}\right) && \text{definition of $\Phi$}\\
             &= \Phi\!\left(\tfrac{d_i+\mu_j\tau_i}{\sigma_j\sqrt{\tau_i}}\right) && \text{rule 4}
\end{align*}
\textbf{Precondition:} Equation~\eqref{eq:mix} uses $\max(\sigma_j,\sigma_{\min})$, which equals $\sigma_j$ because $\sigma_j\ge\sigma_{\min}$.
\textbf{Therefore,} substitution gives $\tilde p_i=P(y_i=1\mid z_i)$. \qed

\paragraph{Lemma~\ref{lem:leak}.}
\textbf{Given:}
1) $\theta=\mathrm{fit}(Z_{\text{train}})$, Baum--Welch on segments only;
2) $(a,b)=\mathrm{Platt}\big(\{\operatorname{logit}\tilde p_j^{\text{oof}}, y_j\}_{j\in\text{train}}\big)$, where each out-of-fold output comes from an HMM fitted on segments only;
3) for a held-out episode, $p_i=g(z_i,d_i,\tau_i;\theta,a,b)$ by Equations~\eqref{eq:filter}--\eqref{eq:platt}.

\textbf{Choose:} a held-out episode $i$ and write $p_i$ through the fitted quantities of rules 1 and 2.

\textbf{Need to prove:} $p_i$ depends on labels only through $\{y_j\}_{j\in\text{train}}$.
\begin{align*}
p_i &= g(z_i,d_i,\tau_i;\,\theta,\,a,\,b) && \text{rule 3}\\
    &= g\big(z_i,d_i,\tau_i;\,\mathrm{fit}(Z_{\text{train}}),\,\mathrm{Platt}(\{\tilde p_j^{\text{oof}},y_j\}_{j\in\text{train}})\big) && \text{rules 1 and 2}
\end{align*}
The only label symbols on the right are $y_j$ with $j\in\text{train}$.

\textbf{Precondition:} a training label must not itself carry held-out information.
A training label reads $X(t_j+\tau_j)$; rows with $t_j+\tau_j$ inside the held-out period are dropped (the embargo of Section~\ref{sec:setup}), so every remaining $y_j$ is a function of $X$ before the held-out period.

\textbf{Therefore,} no held-out label enters $p_i$. The out-of-fold step is not needed for this; it only makes the calibrator see raw outputs from an HMM that did not fit those rows, as at test time. \qed

\paragraph{Lemma~\ref{lem:floor}.}
\textbf{Given:}
1) generalised EM \citep{dempster1977}: if each update satisfies $Q(\theta^{\text{new}}\mid\theta^{\text{old}})\ge Q(\theta^{\text{old}}\mid\theta^{\text{old}})$, the log-likelihood does not decrease;
2) for diagonal or spherical covariances $Q$ separates into one term per state and coordinate, and the variance term has the form $q(v)=-\tfrac12\,(\nu\log v + s/v)$ with weight $\nu>0$ and scatter $s>0$ (a conjugate variance prior only changes $\nu$ and $s$);
3) the mean update does not depend on $v$.

\textbf{Choose:} one variance term $q$ and the feasible set $v\ge m$.

\textbf{Need to prove:} $\arg\max_{v\ge m} q(v)=\max(s/\nu,\,m)$.
\begin{align*}
q'(v) &= -\tfrac12\left(\tfrac{\nu}{v}-\tfrac{s}{v^2}\right) && \text{derivative of rule 2}\\
      &= \tfrac{\nu}{2v^2}\left(\tfrac{s}{\nu}-v\right) && \text{factor out } \tfrac{\nu}{2v^2}>0
\end{align*}
So $q'>0$ for $v<s/\nu$ and $q'<0$ for $v>s/\nu$: $q$ is unimodal with peak $v^\ast=s/\nu=v^{\mathrm{EM}}$.
Same method on each side of $m$.
\textbf{Case $v^\ast\ge m$:}
\begin{align*}
\arg\max_{v\ge m} q(v) &= v^\ast && \text{peak is feasible, unimodal $q$}\\
                       &= \max(v^\ast,m) && v^\ast\ge m
\end{align*}
\textbf{Case $v^\ast<m$:}
\begin{align*}
\arg\max_{v\ge m} q(v) &= m && q'<0 \text{ on } [m,\infty) \text{ since } m>v^\ast\\
                       &= \max(v^\ast,m) && v^\ast<m
\end{align*}
Substitution gives update~\eqref{eq:floor} in both cases.

\textbf{Precondition:} rule 1 needs $\theta^{\text{old}}$ feasible, so that it is a candidate of the constrained maximisation.
The initial variances carry $+m$, so they are feasible.
Every later variance is the output of~\eqref{eq:floor}, so it is $\ge m$.
By induction every iterate is feasible.

\textbf{Therefore,} by rule 3 the means keep their usual update, each variance gets its exact constrained maximiser, $Q$ does not decrease, and by rule 1 neither does the log-likelihood. \qed

\section{Ablations and post-hoc variants}
\label{app:variants}

The benchmark arm equals the public \texttt{RegimeExceedanceClassifier} with a maximum logit difference of \nFxApiDiff.
The lookback edges are \nCfgFxEdges{} minutes (EUR/USD), \nCfgEqEdges{} days (equities) and \nCfgMfEdges{} hours (M4).
The tie on M4 for $K=\nCfgKthree$ has interval \nMfKthreeLo{} to \nMfKthreeHi.
Turning drift on is a large loss on equities and M4; behind \texttt{drift=True} the segment mean is drift per $\sqrt{\text{time}}$ while Equation~\eqref{eq:mix} reads it per unit time.
Neither floor binds on the public data: the smallest stock-EM variance is \nFxCollapseRatio, \nEqCollapseRatio{} and \nMfCollapseRatio{} times the floor, so both ablations are bit-identical.
The floor's evidence is synthetic.

\section{Applying regimix to new data, and reproduction}
\label{app:use}

\paragraph{Inputs.}
One observed path (or many), the decision times, a threshold per decision and a horizon.
Divide the path by the spread of its one-step increments on the training part, so that segments and distances are in unit scale.
Pick lookback edges that roughly double, for example $0,1,3,7,15,31$ steps, with the oldest covering a few horizons.
Keep episodes in time order: the out-of-fold calibration needs a splitter of contiguous time blocks, and the held-out set must come after the training set with an embargo of one horizon.

\paragraph{Calls.}
\texttt{Z = segments\_from\_path(x, times, t, edges)} builds Equation~\eqref{eq:seg};
\texttt{clf = RegimeExceedanceClassifier(n\_states=2).fit(Z, y, distance=d, horizon=h)} fits the HMM and the calibrator, with $d=X(t)-k$;
\texttt{clf.predict\_proba(Z, distance=d, horizon=h)} and \texttt{clf.regime\_posterior(Z)} return $p_i$ and $w_i$;
\texttt{diagnostics.report(clf, Z, y=y, distance=d, horizon=h)} prints the warnings of Equation~\eqref{eq:diag}.
If the report warns on dwell or spread, the regimes are not real and $K=1$ or a realised-vol probit is the better choice, as on M4.
Always run a realised-vol probit beside it: on our data it was the arm to beat.
\texttt{examples/quickstart.py} runs all of this on synthetic data with a known answer.

\paragraph{Reproduction.}
Package, benchmark and paper live in the \texttt{regimix} repository; the results in \texttt{bench/results/} are from commit \texttt{a6e9ca4}.
The whole delta to upstream is \texttt{patches/hmmlearn/0001-gaussianhmm-apply-min-covar-after-every-m-step.patch} (and a spherical-shape fix, \texttt{0002}) against hmmlearn 0.3.3, with repro tests in \texttt{patches/hmmlearn/tests}.
\texttt{bash bench/run\_all.sh} downloads the data and writes \texttt{bench/results/\{fx,eq,m4\}[\_variants].json} (about 1 hour on 2 threads);
\texttt{python results/make\_numbers.py} regenerates every number in this paper and \texttt{python figures/make.py} both figures (Figure~\ref{fig:reliability} reads the held-out predictions the benchmark stores outside git).
